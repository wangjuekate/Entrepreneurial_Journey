02 / What worked well

Five strengths to preserve

  1. Positive, enthusiastic teaching

    Keep the energy, kindness, and passion students appreciated—even at 8 a.m.

  2. Support and responsiveness

    Continue questions, individual help during activities, and flexibility when personal circumstances arise.

  3. Practical, hands-on learning

    Preserve simulations and real-world cases that connect theory to business decisions and future careers.

  4. Interaction and collaboration

    Keep discussions and group activities that bring different perspectives; retain the value students found in panels through more accessible participation formats.

  5. Helpful materials and routines

    Continue useful slides, articles, pre-class blogs, predictable deadlines, and lectures that support assignments.

03 / Improvement 1 of 6 • Assignment clarity • Problem

Instructions and expectations need to be easier to follow.

What students told us

Students asked for simpler, consolidated directions, examples, and checklists—especially for simulations, the midterm, and the final. They also wanted clearer class objectives and in-class project reviews.

03 / Improvement 1 of 6 • Assignment clarity • Solution

Make time to explain the work

01

Dedicated explanation time

Reserve 30 minutes for simulation and final-project explanations.

02

Make expectations visible

Use that time to walk through the workflow, deliverables, and questions.

03 / Improvement 2 of 6 • Group work • Problem

Large-class teamwork needs more structure.

What students told us

Students asked for stable groups, clear meeting locations, and easier ways to find and contact teammates. Uneven participation left some students dependent on absent or unprepared peers.

03 / Improvement 2 of 6 • Group work • Solution

Stable teams. Clear responsibility.

01

Keep teams stable

Maintain the same teams from now on.

02

Allow leader coverage

Team leaders can take over a member’s task when needed.

03

Evaluate contributions

Use evaluations from both the team leader and teammates.

03 / Improvement 3 of 6 • Simulation experience • Problem

SkillSimm needs clearer guidance.

What students told us

Students reported mixed experiences with SkillSimm and asked for clearer demonstrations before activities and deeper debriefs that connect decisions to course concepts.

03 / Improvement 3 of 6 • Simulation experience • Solution

Make the simulation easier to navigate

01

Before the activity

Send email reminders and present the workflow first.

02

During the activity

Use simplified task cards presented by AI and assign materials to each role so students see the materials relevant to their work.

03

Account flexibility

Allow email swapping when needed.

03 / Improvement 4 of 6 • Weekly assignments • Problem

Repetition is reducing the value of weekly work.

What students told us

Several students found reflections and simulation tasks repetitive. They asked for different formats and a clearer explanation of each activity’s learning purpose.

03 / Improvement 4 of 6 • Weekly assignments • Solution

Vary readings and focus reflections

01

Broaden the reading

Replace existing weekly assignments with varied readings from the Financial Times and The Wall Street Journal.

02

Shorten the writing

Reduce the length of simulation reflections.

03

Make feedback more specific

Add a reflection survey to help the instructor give more targeted feedback.

03 / Improvement 5 of 6 • Participation • Problem

Speaking on a panel does not work for everyone.

What students told us

Some students valued panels, while others found them anxiety-inducing. Students requested more small-group discussion and alternative ways to contribute.

03 / Improvement 5 of 6 • Participation • Solution

Write first. Share from your seat.

01

Replace debrief panels

Remove the panel format from debriefs.

02

Prepare a written response

Write answers on a participation card.

03

Contribute from your seat

Present your response while remaining in your seat.

03 / Improvement 6 of 6 • Pacing and depth • Problem

Students need more time to process—and want more depth.

What students told us

Students asked for slower explanations and slides, more discussion time, and accessible recaps. Some also wanted practical business-startup guidance and opportunities to interact with entrepreneurs.

03 / Improvement 6 of 6 • Pacing and depth • Solution

Slow down and point toward deeper learning

01

Pause for understanding

Slow down explanations and ask questions before moving ahead.

02

Explore startup guidance

Take a more in-depth course, listed as “MEMT 3235.”
