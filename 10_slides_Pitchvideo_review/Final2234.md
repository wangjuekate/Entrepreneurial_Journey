## Individual option for students without a team

**Team participation is recommended.** A team can divide the five report sections, research, calculations, and presentation preparation among five members, then review and integrate the work together. An individual student is responsible for all five areas and the entire recording. The individual deliverables are shorter, but the work per student is greater because the core analysis is completed independently.

Students without a team may use this option with either venture path in Section 1. **Use the individual rubric and checklist below; the requirements in Sections 2–4 and the checklist in Section 6 apply to teams.** Section 5's source and citation guidance applies to both options. Both options have the same deadlines, maximum points, and course weight.

- **Report:** Aim for **6–8 pages of main text**, plus a title page, references, and the concise application appendix described below. This is a suggested length, not a separate grading criterion. Include a brief executive summary and all five rubric areas.
- **Evidence:** Use at least **five credible sources**, including the selected program's official requirements and evidence about customers and competing alternatives. Cite claims, show calculations, and distinguish evidence from assumptions. Published research is sufficient; interviews, surveys, a working prototype, and an external application remain optional.
- **Scope:** Keep the venture focused on one initial customer segment and one proposed offering. Complete every core analysis in the individual rubric. Concise tables and calculations are appropriate; each recommendation needs evidence and an explanation.
- **Application preparation appendix:** Include a concise **1–2-page appendix** with the program's official link and date checked, eligibility and your fit, current application window, required materials, one drafted application response or service inquiry, and remaining preparation steps. Summarize program fit and readiness in the pitch. This work is assessed within the program-fit criterion and carries no additional points.
- **Pitch video:** Record an **8–10-minute** presentation yourself covering all five rubric areas. Explain your evidence, calculations, choices, and support request. Use readable visuals, audible speech, and source credits.
- **Grading:** The report and video are each worth **100 points**, using the individual rubric separately for each deliverable. The final project remains **20% of the course grade**, with each deliverable contributing **10%**. Each criterion is assessed on the submitted evidence and reasoning; completing the project alone does not waive a criterion.

## 2. Team final report — 100 points

Write a standalone proposal that a reviewer can understand without reading your midterm. Begin with a title page naming the venture and all team members, followed by a concise executive summary covering the customer problem, solution, evidence, business model, and specific request for funding or support.

Organize the body into Sections A–E below, worth **20 points each**. The executive summary and application preparation appendix are assessed within Section E; they do not carry extra points.

### A. Venture Concept and Creative Thinking — 20 points · Team Leader

- **Customer problem and proposed solution — 8 points:** Identify the intended customer and user, the problem they face, and how your product or service addresses it. Explain the value customers would receive.
- **Creative thinking and differentiation — 8 points:** Show how you generated, compared, and improved ideas. Compare your solution with existing alternatives, including how customers currently solve the problem.
- **Feasibility — 4 points:** Describe the current concept, mockup, prototype, or service workflow, and distinguish what exists from what is planned.

### B. Customer Evidence, Pitfalls, and Risk Mitigation — 20 points · Member 1

- **Customer and market evidence — 8 points:** Define an initial customer segment and support the need with credible research, interviews, observations, surveys, or tests. Explain the limits of your evidence and identify assumptions that remain untested.
- **Entrepreneurial pitfalls — 6 points:** Analyze the most relevant risks, such as weak demand, unrealistic pricing, high costs, team gaps, technical uncertainty, or dependence on a partner. Connect your analysis to lessons from the course or simulation.
- **Mitigation and validation — 6 points:** Propose practical tests and safeguards. State what evidence would lead you to proceed, revise the idea, or stop. Assign responsibility for the next tests.

### C. Business Model and Acquisition Evaluation — 20 points · Member 2

- **Business model and economics — 8 points:** Explain who pays, what they pay for, how the venture reaches customers, and the main delivery costs. Provide a simple revenue-and-cost estimate with explicit assumptions; for a noncommercial project, explain its funding and sustainability model.
- **Build, buy, or partner comparison — 6 points:** Compare developing the capability yourselves with acquiring an existing business or assets and working with a partner. Recommend a realistic route for your venture's current stage.
- **Due diligence — 6 points:** Identify what you would investigate before an acquisition or major partnership, including customers, finances, obligations, intellectual property, and operational fit. If acquisition is unsuitable, justify that conclusion with evidence rather than omitting the analysis.

### D. Growth, Franchising, and Partnerships — 20 points · Member 3

- **Initial launch and growth strategy — 8 points:** Explain how you would reach your first customers, deliver the offering, and expand if early tests succeed. Identify useful partners and the value each party would contribute.
- **Franchising assessment — 6 points:** Assess whether the model can be standardized and replicated. Explain the potential franchisor–franchisee relationship, quality control, and relevant regulatory and tax questions. If franchising is unsuitable, explain why and recommend another growth route.
- **Execution milestones — 6 points:** Present a practical next-stage timeline with tasks, owners, resources, and measurable outcomes. Match the scope to your team's capacity and the support you plan to request.

### E. Program Fit, Resource Request, and Professional Presentation — 20 points · Member 4

- **Program fit and application preparation — 6 points:** Identify your primary target program, its purpose, current eligibility requirements, and application format. Explain how your venture meets those requirements, or identify the specific steps needed to become eligible. Include the appendix described below.
- **Funding or support request — 6 points:** State the amount or service requested and what it will accomplish. Include an itemized budget or service scope tied to milestones. Explain how you would proceed if only part of the requested support becomes available.
- **Clarity and consistency — 8 points:** Provide a clear executive summary, coherent sections, accurate citations, consistent figures, and polished writing. The report and video must describe the same venture, evidence, and request.

### Application preparation appendix

Include this appendix in the report PDF so your team can use the work after the course:

| Item | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| Target and source | Program name, official application link, and date you checked the requirements. |
| Eligibility | Relevant affiliation, team-size, venture-stage, or other requirements; explain your fit and any unresolved questions. |
| Application window | Current published deadline or next known cycle. If none is posted, state that it has not been announced. |
| Required materials | Application questions, word limits, attachments, pitch length, live-pitch requirements, and other published instructions. |
| Prepared content | Draft responses to the main application questions or a service inquiry, supported by your report. Identify report sections or video segments that can be reused. |
| Remaining work | List any shorter video edit, form, mentor confirmation, eligibility check, or other step needed before an external submission, with an owner for each. |

For a program with a selection pathway, explain the next eligible step. For example, a team targeting Wolff should explain the Summer Accelerator pathway. For a clinic or consulting program, prepare a focused request for assistance and define the expected output instead of requesting a cash prize.

## 3. Team final pitch video — 10–20 minutes, 100 points

**The Team Leader must submit the video file or an accessible video link to the Blackboard final video assignment by the posted deadline.** Check that the instructor can open and play the submitted version.

Present your venture to the reviewers of your chosen program. Make a clear case for the problem, solution, evidence, execution plan, and request. Use the structure below. The suggested section times total 15 minutes; the required video length is 10–20 minutes.

| Pitch section | Suggested time | Scoring criteria | Points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Problem, customer, and solution | 3 minutes | Clear customer and problem (8); compelling solution and demonstration or concrete example (8); differentiation from alternatives (4). | **20** |
| Evidence and creative thinking | 3 minutes | Credible customer or market evidence (10); explanation of how ideas were generated, compared, and refined (6); honest account of remaining assumptions (4). | **20** |
| Business model and growth choices | 3 minutes | Clear revenue or sustainability model and costs (8); justified build, acquire, or partner decision (6); reasoned franchising or alternative growth assessment (6). | **20** |
| Risks, team, and execution | 3 minutes | Relevant pitfalls and practical mitigation (8); achievable milestones and measures of success (8); clear team roles and capability gaps (4). | **20** |
| Program fit, request, and delivery | 3 minutes | Specific fit with the target program (6); justified funding or support request and intended outcomes (8); clear, professional delivery with readable visuals and audible speech (6). | **20** |
| **Total** | **15 minutes suggested; 10–20 minutes required** | | **100** |

Support claims and figures with the evidence in your report. Label projections, proposed partnerships, and planned tests clearly. Use readable source credits and finish with a concrete request: what support you need, what you will do with it, and what result reviewers should expect.

The 10–20-minute video is the **course version**. In your application appendix, explain how you would adapt it to your target program's requirements. For example, Get Seeded currently uses a five-minute live pitch followed by three minutes of questions; a course recording does not replace that live format. See the [official Get Seeded instructions](https://ccei.uconn.edu/getseeded/).

## 5. Research and sources

Use evidence that helps a reviewer assess your venture. Prioritize customer needs, competing solutions, pricing, delivery costs, and the chosen program's requirements. Cite sources where claims appear and include a reference list with links and dates.

- **Customer research:** Explain your methods and findings. Obtain consent for interviews, attribute insights appropriately, and identify limitations. Do not present planned interviews or invented responses as completed research.
- **Market and competitor research:** Use credible data and current product or pricing information. Show the assumptions behind market estimates and financial projections.
- **Company evidence:** For Option A, draw on relevant filings, company documents, and your midterm analysis. Use [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) when filings help answer a specific question; explain how the evidence supports your proposed venture.
- **Program evidence:** Use official application instructions for eligibility, deadlines, budget rules, and format. Record when you checked them.
- **Expert perspectives:** [UConn NetWerx](https://werth.institute.uconn.edu/netwerx/) can help you identify alumni perspectives on customers, operations, and entrepreneurial trade-offs.
