Six Thousand Against Ninety Thousand: Netflix in 2008

The case

Early 2008. Netflix has 7.5 million subscribers, about 90,000 DVD titles, a nationwide shipping network and about $1.2 billion of revenue. A year ago it launched instant watching — bundled free, 6,000 titles, roughly one fifteenth of the library. Source: FY2007 10-K.

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who had the same role.
  • Before any discussion of answers, draw the Diverge–Converge Loop — defining, ideating, prototyping and testing.
  • Name the stage your decision sits in, and what that means about how fast to move.
  • Select one representative to present your group's placement and draw it on the board.
💰

Financial Lead

Instant watching earns no separate revenue — it is bundled free. Gross margin is 34.8% and fell this year. Every streaming dollar comes out of the disc business. How do you fund it?

  • A Protect a fixed slice of revenue; judge it on what we learn, not what it earns.
  • B Fund it from what streaming saves — every film watched online is a disc not posted twice.
  • C Cap it at a small experiment budget until studio licensing costs are clear.
  • D Wait until instant watching shows real revenue before funding it properly.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Fund a prototype on learning goals, not revenue targets. Instant watching earns nothing by design — that is the experiment, not a defect in it.
  • Gross margin is 34.8% and falling, and every streaming dollar comes out of the disc business that pays for everything.
  • (D) is the trap. With what would it produce the revenue? This is how experiments die everywhere, and it always sounds like discipline.
📣

Marketing Lead

You must describe 6,000 titles to subscribers who already get 90,000 on disc, while Blockbuster attacks the core business. What do you say?

  • A Give it away inside existing plans and watch what people actually watch — the information beats the revenue.
  • B Use it as the reason to stay with us rather than switch to Blockbuster.
  • C Charge separately, to find out what people will pay for watching instantly.
  • D Market it as the replacement for DVDs — get people onto the future now.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Reframe positioning as a learning instrument. Bundled free, the question stops being “what will they pay” and becomes “what do they watch.”
  • 6,000 streaming titles against 90,000 on disc is one fifteenth of the library. What you promise has to fit what you actually have.
  • (D) is the trap. Promising a replacement that delivers one fifteenth of the library manufactures churn and teaches the company that streaming “failed.”
⚙️

Operation Lead

The shipping network moves 90,000 titles to 7.5 million subscribers and is 100% of revenue. Its possible replacement is one year old with 6,000 titles. What do you do with the network?

  • A Keep it at full strength — it pays for everything, including the experiment.
  • B Use streaming for the obscure titles that barely get posted; take that cost out of shipping.
  • C Consolidate some shipping centres, put the savings into streaming rights.
  • D Start winding it down now — streaming is obviously where this ends up.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Protect the cash engine that funds the experiment. The shipping network is 100% of revenue and serves 7.5 million subscribers.
  • The long tail is the bridge: titles that barely get posted are the cheapest thing to move online first, and the saving is real.
  • (D) is the trap. Pure hindsight. On 2008 evidence this dismantles the only source of revenue in order to fund a PC-only feature with 6,000 titles.
💻

Tech Lead

Instant watching runs only on PCs — and almost nobody watches films at a desk. In January 2008 the company announced an LG arrangement, terms not finalised. Where should this run?

  • A One service any device can plug into; treat the PC as the prototype it is.
  • B Partner with TV makers like LG to reach the screen people actually watch films on.
  • C Build our own box that plugs into the television, controlling the whole experience.
  • D Perfect it on the PC and wait until streaming demand is proven before building elsewhere.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • A PC-only test is not a valid test. Almost nobody watches films at a desk, so the experiment measures the wrong thing.
  • The device question has to be settled before anyone measures demand — otherwise every number that follows is about the screen, not the service.
  • (D) is the trap. A rigged test dressed as discipline. It measures appetite for films on the one screen where people do not watch films.
🔒

Security Lead

Streaming adds two things to protect: licensed films the studios will pull if they leak, and a record of what every subscriber watched. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Protect both above all else — the films, or licensing stops; and viewing history, because it's nobody else's business.
  • B Full content protection before broadening distribution to more devices.
  • C Rely on device partners to handle protection on their own hardware.
  • D Deal with it once streaming is a material part of the business.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Rank two asymmetric risks rather than treating security as one dial: leaked films end the licences, leaked viewing history harms subscribers.
  • Content protection gates distribution — you cannot reach more devices than the rights holders will tolerate.
  • (D) is the trap. By the time streaming is material, the licences and the data are already exposed.
🧭

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question. After all five leads present their selection and a one-sentence defence:

  • 1 Rank the five problems 1–5, each used once, with one sentence justifying #1 that contains a figure from the packet.
  • 2 Weight them — 100 points across the five, in blocks of 10, totalling exactly 100.

Part 3 — Name the stage of the loop. Defining, ideating, or prototyping and testing? And what does that mean about how fast the company should move?

🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Ranking is order; weighting is magnitude. A flat split scores 2 of 6 — the designed penalty for refusing to choose.
  • The strong pattern ranks devices and funding above positioning and rights, keeps the DVD network off the bottom, and weights the top two at about 70 of 100.
  • The company is still at prototype-and-test — which means move deliberately, not fast. Naming the stage is what sets the speed.

Key takeaway

Creativity is a loop, not a moment. The discipline is knowing which stage you are in — and refusing to run your test on the one screen where nobody watches films.

Fund a prototype on what it teaches, not on what it earns.
