Six Thousand Against Ninety Thousand: Netflix in 2008

The case

Early 2008. Netflix has 7.5 million subscribers, about 90,000 DVD titles, a nationwide shipping network and about $1.2 billion of revenue. A year ago it launched instant watching — bundled free, 6,000 titles, roughly one fifteenth of the library. Source: FY2007 10-K.

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who had the same role.
  • Draw the Diverge–Converge Loop — defining, ideating, prototyping and testing.
  • Name the stage your decision sits in, and what that means about how fast to move.
  • Cite a figure from your packet, name what your choice gives up, and state one thing that would change your answer.
  • Select one representative to present your group's placement and reasoning on the board.
💰

Financial Lead

Instant watching earns no separate revenue. Gross margin is 34.8% and fell this year. Every dollar spent on streaming content comes out of a DVD business that pays for everything. How do you fund it?

  • A Cap it at a small experiment budget until we know what the studios will charge for streaming rights.
  • B Wait until instant watching shows real revenue before putting proper money behind it.
  • C Protect a fixed slice of revenue for it and judge it on what we learn, not on what it earns.
  • D Fund it from what streaming saves us — every film watched online is a disc we don't post twice.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Funding follows a question. Explain what uncertainty your spending would resolve and how the result would affect the next round of the loop.
  • Set a review point. Identify a budget, time horizon and measure of progress appropriate to the stage you selected.
  • Account for the tradeoff. Explain how your proposal affects the existing business and which assumption would change your allocation.
📣

Marketing Lead

You have to describe a feature with about 6,000 titles to subscribers who already get about 90,000 on disc, while Blockbuster attacks your core business. What do you say about instant watching?

  • A Use it as the reason to stay with us rather than switch to Blockbuster.
  • B Charge for it separately, so we find out what people will actually pay for watching instantly.
  • C Give it away inside the plans people already have and watch what they actually watch — right now the information is worth more than the revenue.
  • D Market it as the replacement for DVDs — get people onto the future now.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Positioning shapes the experiment. A message or price changes who tries the service and what their behavior can tell you. State what your proposal would test.
  • Separate questions about demand. Interest, usage, retention and willingness to pay measure different things. Explain which matters for your current decision.
  • Return to the problem definition. Describe a finding that would make you rethink the customer problem rather than simply rewrite the campaign.
⚙️

Operation Lead

You run a nationwide shipping network moving about 90,000 titles to 7.5 million subscribers — the entire revenue of the company. The thing that might replace it is a year old and has 6,000 titles. What do you do with the network?

  • A Keep the shipping network at full strength — it pays for everything, including the experiment.
  • B Start winding the network down now — streaming is obviously where this ends up.
  • C Consolidate some shipping centres and put the savings into streaming rights.
  • D Use streaming for the obscure titles that barely get posted, and take that cost out of shipping.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Two activities share resources. Explain how your network decision affects current service and the ability to explore a new one.
  • Test the assumptions behind a transition. Identify what must be true about demand, costs and service quality for your proposal to work.
  • Use the return arrow. State what you would learn from the change and when that evidence would prompt another decision.

Tech Lead

Instant watching runs on personal computers. Almost nobody watches a film at a desk. In January 2008 the company announced a development arrangement with LG Electronics. Where should this run, and what do you build?

  • A Partner with television makers like LG to get this onto the screen people actually watch films on.
  • B Build one service that any device can plug into, and treat the PC as the prototype it is — not the product.
  • C Build our own box that plugs into the television, so we control the whole experience.
  • D Perfect it on the PC and wait until streaming demand is proven before building for anything else.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Test conditions shape what you learn. Consider how the device, catalog and viewing context affect the behavior you observe. Name what your proposed test can and cannot establish.
  • Diverge before selecting a design. Compare the assumptions behind the available technical approaches, then explain the criteria used to choose.
  • Make failure informative. Identify a result that would challenge your design and explain how it would change the next prototype.
🔒

Security Lead

Streaming means protecting two new things: films the studios will pull if they leak, and a record of what every subscriber watched. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Get full content protection in place before we broaden distribution to more devices.
  • B Rely on the device partners to handle protection on their own hardware.
  • C Deal with it properly once streaming is actually a material part of the business.
  • D Protect two things above everything else — the studios' films, or they stop licensing to us, and what each subscriber watched, because that list is nobody else's business.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Experiments involve stakeholders. Identify how a trial affects subscribers, rights holders and device partners, including those who do not choose its design.
  • Define the boundaries of the test. Explain what safeguards and responsibilities your proposal requires, and who checks that they are in place.
  • Balance learning with exposure. Describe what information the trial needs, the risks it introduces and the conditions for expanding or stopping it.

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question. After the five leads present, consider devices, funding, the DVD network, positioning, and content rights and viewing data.

  • 1 Rank the problems 1–5 by what must be resolved first for instant watching to develop. Use each once. Justify #1 in one sentence containing a figure from your packet.
  • 2 Allocate 100 points across the five problems in blocks of 10, totalling exactly 100.
  • 3 Name the stage of the loop. Define, ideate, or prototype and test? Explain which stage the company is at and what that means for how fast it should move.
🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Stage and pace need evidence. Explain your placement on the loop from the 2007 filing and what it implies for the next commitment.
  • Priorities coordinate the experiment. Ranking sets order; weighting expresses relative emphasis. Show how your choices work together to answer the next important question.
  • Avoid reasoning from the ending. Name a result that would make your team redefine the problem. Defend the plan using information available in the case.

Key takeaway

The Diverge–Converge Loop connects a problem definition to options, a test and a revised question. Explain what your team would learn next, how it would recognize a failed assumption, and what it would change.
