Organize Part 1 of your report into the following four sections.

2. Analyze the personal and professional trade-offs entrepreneurs face.
3. Reflect on how your simulation experiences connect to the entrepreneurial journey.

### B. Ethical and Legal Framework — 15 points

1. Identify the ethical standards relevant to your chosen company.

3. Evaluate privacy, security, and data protection considerations.

### C. Corporate Entrepreneurship Assessment — 15 points + up to 2 bonus points

Corporate entrepreneurship, or intrapreneurship, involves developing new ideas and ventures within an existing organization.

1. Analyze intrapreneurial opportunities within your chosen company.
2. Evaluate organizational structures that support innovation.
3. Assess how resources are secured and allocated to support new initiatives.
4. **Bonus:** Up to 2 points are available for a comprehensive analysis.

### D. Social Entrepreneurship Evaluation — 15 points

1. Define the company’s social mission and the problems it seeks to address.
2. Analyze hybrid business model approaches that combine social and commercial goals.
3. Evaluate methods for measuring social impact.
4. Assess strategies for maintaining financial sustainability.

## Research and sources

### SEC filings: compare the filing sequence

Use [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) to find available filings. When an IPO filing sequence is available, collect the initial **S-1**, its **S-1/A amendments**, and the **424B4 final prospectus**. The number of amendments may vary.

Compare consecutive versions and look for changes in:

- **Financial information:** revised figures, additional reporting periods, or changes to non-GAAP definitions.
- **Risk disclosures:** risks that were added, clarified, or given greater emphasis.
- **Business descriptions:** changes in how the company describes its activities and strategy.
- **Customer concentration:** new or more detailed information about reliance on particular customers.

Explain what the changes suggest and support your interpretation with evidence. Avoid assuming that a change resulted from SEC or underwriter pressure unless your sources establish that connection. These comparisons can strengthen your midterm analysis of legal compliance, governance, and financial sustainability, as well as your final analysis of risks and growth strategies.

Other useful filings include **10-K annual reports**, **10-Q quarterly reports**, **8-K current reports**, and **DEF 14A proxy statements**, which can provide information about governance, board structure, and compensation.

### Alumni interviews

[UConn NetWerx](https://werth.institute.uconn.edu/netwerx/) can help you connect with alumni. Interviews can add firsthand perspectives on entrepreneurial rewards, challenges, and trade-offs. Obtain consent, take careful notes, and attribute interview insights appropriately in your report.

### Additional evidence

Consider company annual letters, policy and engineering blogs, transparency reports, and governance documents. Consult relevant regulatory and industry materials, such as consumer lending requirements for Affirm, aviation and export-control requirements for SpaceX, payment security standards for Stripe, and AI policy frameworks for Anthropic.

Reputable business reporting can help document funding rounds and valuations. Cite the outlet and publication date, and distinguish reported valuations from audited financial information. Cite your sources throughout the report so readers can follow your evidence.
