MoviePass at 197%

The case

Spring 2018. Helios and Matheson Analytics owns a majority of MoviePass. In August 2017 the price was cut to $9.95 a month for a movie a day; within four months there were over a million paying subscribers. Assess that growth alongside the costs and commitments in the filing. Source: FY2017 10-K.

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who had the same role.
  • Draw the Decision Pattern grid — reversibility against consequence.
  • Put your decision in a box: one-way door or two-way door? Then decide at the speed that box allows.
  • Cite a figure from your packet, name what your choice gives up, and state one thing that would change your answer.
  • Select one representative to present your group's placement and reasoning on the board.
💰

Financial Lead

You have $24.9 million in the bank, the company burned $27.4 million running itself last year, and cost of revenues is 197% of revenue. Where does the next dollar go?

  • A Cut operating expenses hard — $45.9 M of them against $10.4 M of revenue.
  • B Fix what a subscription costs and what it earns before spending another dollar on acquisition.
  • C Raise capital now while the story is hot, and use the time to fix the economics.
  • D Keep buying subscribers; at enough scale the theaters have to offer a better deal.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Classify the commitment. Place the proposed use of cash on both axes: reversibility and consequence if wrong. Explain what could be recovered if the plan fails.
  • Separate urgency from certainty. Identify the information essential to this decision and what waiting for it would cost.
  • Test the assumptions behind survival. Distinguish cash available, the rate of spending and the economics of serving customers. State how a change in one affects your plan.
📣

Marketing Lead

A million people signed up in four months — and every one of them loses money at $9.95. What do you do with the marketing budget?

  • A Stop paying to acquire loss-making subscribers; move to a price that covers the cost to serve.
  • B Push $9.95 harder — the subscriber number is the story; go for the next milestone.
  • C Keep growing but push annual prepay, to hold the cash up front.
  • D Split the plans — cheap tier for light users, higher price for heavy users.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • A campaign changes expectations. Explain which parts of your proposal can be changed quickly and which customer commitments may persist.
  • Choose the evidence for the decision. Identify what sign-ups, usage, retention and cash receipts each tell you, and what they leave unresolved.
  • Match scope to reversibility. Describe a bounded test, the consequences if it fails and what would be needed to undo it.
⚙️

Operation Lead

Cost of revenues rose 323% because MoviePass buys most tickets at full retail and hands them to subscribers. What do you do about the cost of the product itself?

  • A Renegotiate ticket economics with the theater chains before adding anyone else.
  • B Keep paying full price — at enough audience share the theaters will come to us.
  • C Concentrate on cities where partner deals already exist; stop serving the rest.
  • D Cap how many movies a subscriber can see, to bound the worst case per member.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Operational choices depend on others. Identify which changes the team controls and which require agreement from theaters or customers.
  • Assess both axes. Compare the consequence of a failed cost assumption with the practical difficulty of reversing your proposal.
  • Define the decision process. State what evidence and consultation the choice needs, who decides and when it should be reviewed.
💻

Tech Lead

The app handles sign-up, theater check-in, and the data the company keeps calling its real asset. Cash is short. What do you build and what do you buy?

  • A Build the ticketing integrations with theaters, because those cut the cost per ticket.
  • B Ship consumer features that drive sign-ups; instrument the economics later.
  • C Build real-time visibility into what each subscriber costs — right now nobody can see it.
  • D Buy analytics off the shelf to get numbers this month instead of next quarter.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Information has value when it changes action. Specify the decision your proposed build or purchase would improve and how soon the information is needed.
  • Include switching costs. Consider implementation time, dependence on suppliers and disruption when placing the proposal in the grid.
  • Distinguish essential from desirable. Explain what the team must know before committing and what it can learn through a limited trial.
🔒

Security Lead

Subscribers hand over a payment card and check in from their phone at the theater — so you hold card details and location history, while the auditors are questioning whether the company survives. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Push the obligation onto the third-party suppliers who run the systems, via contracts.
  • B Defer until the company is making money; live with the exposure until then.
  • C Full investment now — you hold card details for a million people.
  • D Risk-based: payment cards and location history first; lower-severity controls wait.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Consequences extend beyond the company. Identify potential harm to subscribers as well as operational and financial effects when assessing severity.
  • Evaluate what can be reversed. Explain which changes your plan could undo and which consequences might remain even after a system is fixed.
  • Make responsibilities actionable. Identify who implements, checks and responds to controls across company and supplier boundaries, given the available resources.
🧭

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question. After the five leads present, consider money, ticket costs, subscriber data, growth, and tech.

  • 1 Rank the problems 1–5 by what the company must resolve to survive. Use each once. Justify #1 in one sentence containing a figure from your packet.
  • 2 Allocate 100 points across the five problems in blocks of 10, totalling exactly 100.
  • 3 Name the one-way door. Which of the five decisions cannot be walked back? Explain what makes it difficult to reverse and the consequences if it is wrong.
🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Different decisions need different processes. Explain the grid placement of each action rather than assigning one speed to the whole company.
  • Priorities need a survival argument. Ranking sets order; weighting expresses relative emphasis. Support both with figures and dependencies among the five decisions.
  • Resolve disagreement deliberately. Distinguish uncertainty about evidence from disagreement about goals. Explain when to commit, when to seek more information and when to escalate.

Key takeaway

Return to the student club choosing a speaker and a poster. Use reversibility and consequence to explain which process each decision needs, what information is essential, and who can authorize action.

If your decision is wrong, what would it take to walk it back?
