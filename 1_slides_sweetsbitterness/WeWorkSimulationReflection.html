WeWork at the Edge

The panel

  • Join with classmates who have the same role.
  • As a group of the same role, discuss about your decisions and reasons.
  • Select one representative to come and sit on the front for a interview about what we have learned.
💰

Financial Lead

Location opex is 120% of revenue and FY2021 net loss was $4.6B. Where should the next dollar of scarce cash go?

  • A Keep opening already-signed locations to reach scale and lift occupancy.
  • B Halt new openings; direct all cash to lease exits / restructurings.
  • C Split — open only locations already above break-even; exit the rest.
  • D Raise capital now and stay the course, accepting dilution.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • At 120% opex, every additional filled building deepens the loss — occupancy rising 46%→65% improved the ratio, not the outcome.
  • Scale only fixes unit economics if the marginal location clears break-even; otherwise the next dollar buys more of the same loss.
  • The decisive number is contribution margin per location — not total revenue, not headline occupancy.
📣

Marketing Lead

All Access grew 254% (13K→46K) while physical memberships fell 18% (505K→416K). Where should marketing spend go?

  • A Push flexible All Access hard — it's the only line growing.
  • B Defend physical / enterprise memberships — they're the revenue base.
  • C Prioritize retention over acquisition given churn and cancellations.
  • D Rebrand around the post-Neumann governance turnaround.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Growth rates mislead: +254% on 13K adds ~33K members, while −18% on 505K loses ~89K. The base matters more than the rate.
  • When churn drives the decline, a retained member is cheaper than an acquired one.
  • Every focus has a cost — funding the fastest-growing line defunds the revenue base.
⚙️

Operation Lead

Leases run to 2034, but committed member revenue drops $720.6M (2022) → $457.1M (2023). How do you manage the portfolio?

  • A Exit / restructure the weakest markets first (real-estate optimization).
  • B Consolidate members into fewer, fuller buildings to lift occupancy.
  • C Convert underused space to on-demand / All Access instead of exiting.
  • D Hold everything — occupancy is recovering; wait for demand.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The commitment is asymmetric: obligations run to 2034, member commitments run one year and are halving — WeWork absorbs the gap.
  • Exit, consolidate, and convert each pay a different price: cash and market presence, member experience, or revenue certainty.
  • Sequence by where being wrong is survivable — weakest markets first, anchor markets last.
💻

Tech Lead

The app is proprietary but built on third-party and open-source software. With cash scarce, how do you invest in the platform?

  • A Buy / lease third-party platforms — cheapest now, fastest to occupancy.
  • B Build in-house — control and differentiation, but slower and costlier.
  • C Hybrid — keep the member-facing app proprietary, outsource commodity back-office.
  • D Freeze platform investment until the company is cash-positive.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Only the member-facing experience differentiates; the back office is a commodity anyone can rent.
  • Cheapest today can be dearest later — vendor lock-in defers cost, it does not remove it.
  • Freezing platform spend is a false economy when the platform is what makes flexible / All Access sellable.
🔒

Security Lead

WeWork holds members' data, is bound by privacy law, and admits it "may be unable to anticipate" new attacks — while every budget is cut. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Full investment now — a member-data breach ends the turnaround.
  • B Risk-based minimum — protect member/payment data first, defer the rest.
  • C Shift risk to vendors via contracts / SLAs.
  • D Defer security spend until solvent; accept the interim exposure.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • A member-data breach is not a line item — it ends the turnaround the other four decisions are trying to fund.
  • Under a hard budget, protection is ranked rather than uniform: member and payment data first.
  • Contracts and SLAs move liability, not risk — the reputational loss stays with WeWork.
🧭

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question.

After all five leads present their selection + one-sentence defense, rank the five decisions 1–5 by which WeWork must resolve first to survive — and give the single sentence that justifies rank #1.

🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Rank by survival impact and evidence — not by whoever argued loudest.
  • A decision no one can defend with a number cannot credibly be #1.
  • Unanimity among the five leads is a warning, not a confirmation — shared optimism is how the overexpansion happened.

Key takeaway

Managing emotions is part of managing the company. Better emotional discipline leads to better strategic discipline.

Emotion is the weather. The numbers are the map.
