The Sweets & the Bitterness of Entrepreneurship

MENT 2234 · Module 1 · Concept Lecture

It is not a straight line up

Everybody thinks entrepreneurship is a line going up and to the right.

It is a roller coaster — and the tickets are non-refundable.

Same ride, same people: some get off at the top, some at the bottom.

Roller-coaster curve of a founder's journey: the same track carries both the highs (sweets) and the drops (bitterness).

A true story — the sweets

At 27, broke and sleeping on a friend's couch, two founders rented out air mattresses in their apartment — cereal included — just to make rent.

To fund it, they sold novelty cereal boxes at $40 a box.

Ten years on: a company worth more than the entire Hilton chain — owning none of the buildings. That is Airbnb.

Figure — the origin story
Airbnb's cereal-box / air-mattress beginning next to its later valuation — the "started from nothing" contrast.
The couch-surfing founder becomes a billionaire; owns the app that owns the idea, not the buildings. Sets up the three sweets.

What are the returns?

Financial freedom

Not "rich" — free. A salary has a number on it; a company you own does not.

Fulfillment

The first time a stranger pays for something that did not exist until you made it — that vote hits different.

To be remembered

Disney was told he "lacked imagination." Founders try to leave a dent in the universe.

Freedom, meaning, immortality. That's the pitch — then turn the lights down.

The flip side — the $47 billion man

Adam Neumann's WeWork was "worth" $47B. He rang the bell early and said the party would never end.

The moment strangers opened the books, $47B became ~$7B in weeks. The IPO collapsed; he was pushed out.

He left with a ~$1B parachute. 2,000 employees lost their jobs and their stock. Same ride — different exits.

What are the risks?

Financial risk

The venture can fail; founders can go bankrupt. A personal guarantee has your name on it.

Career risk

Founders often have to leave a stable job to pursue the venture — with no guarantee of return.

Family risk

Time and attention get pulled to the business; relationships strain under the load.

Mental risk

Stress and loneliness; no off switch. The phone becomes a leash.

These four dimensions are the axes of the risk/return tool a few slides ahead.

The bitterness, up close

Deep in debt

WeWork lost $4.6B in a single year and spent more to run its buildings than it earned. Sign a lease guarantee and failure follows you home.

The slaved schedule

No weekend, no off switch. Being your own boss can mean hiring the worst boss you've had — one who never lets you clock out.

Regret

Sold too early, or held on too long. Falling in love with your own story is the most expensive romance there is.

The upside is capped by ambition; the downside is capped by your signature.

A tool to weigh risk vs. return

Plot a venture on the curve: how much financial / career / family / mental risk buys how much return.

The one line to carry in

The sweet and the bitter are not two rides. They are the same ride.

At every turn, solve the problem in front of you — do not surrender to the emotion the turn makes you feel.

Emotion is the weather. The numbers are the map.

WeWork's founder felt like a genius at $47B and never looked at the numbers. Airbnb's founders felt like failures eating cereal and looked every day.

Your job is not to feel about it. Your job is to decide.
