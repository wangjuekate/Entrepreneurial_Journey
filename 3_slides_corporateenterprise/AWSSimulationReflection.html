The Venture Inside: AWS in 2008

The case

Early 2008. Amazon has just filed its FY2007 annual report: $14.8 billion in net sales, $655 million from operations — a 4.4% margin. Eighteen months ago it launched Amazon Web Services, sold to a customer the filing has to introduce as “developer customers.”

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who had the same role.
  • Draw the CEAI chain — trigger, internal conditions, middle manager, outcome.
  • Place your decision on the chain and say which link it depends on.
  • Cite a figure from your packet, name what your choice gives up, and state one thing that would change your answer.
  • Select one representative to present your group's placement and reasoning on the board.
💰

Financial Lead

Technology and content spend has risen from $451 million to $818 million in two years, partly on AWS. The company's operating margin is 4.4%, and AWS revenue is not reported separately anywhere in this filing. How does this business get funded and measured?

  • A A protected central innovation budget, ring-fenced so retail's targets can't raid it.
  • B Funded out of retail's operating budget and held to the same margin everyone else hits.
  • C Its own P&L and capital budget, measured against outside cloud competitors — not retail.
  • D No dedicated funding until it shows enough revenue to justify it.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Funding shapes internal conditions. A budget also establishes decision rights and review expectations. Explain how your choice affects management support, discretion and time to experiment.
  • Connect measures to outcomes. The lecture asks you to benchmark twice: against competitors and against the company's own prior years. Explain what each comparison reveals and what remains unknown.
  • Identify the manager in the middle. Who must defend the investment at the next review? State the evidence they would need and the tradeoff they would be accepting.
📣

Marketing Lead

The filing describes a new category — "developer customers" who can use AWS to "enable virtually any type of business." Amazon has never sold to developers. Where does the effort go?

  • A Sell through the enterprise relationships we already have from our seller services business.
  • B Go after startups and small companies who could never afford their own data centre.
  • C Market it to the customers we already have — the people who buy books and electronics from us.
  • D Make it something a developer can try in ten minutes without talking to anyone — docs, self-serve signup, a free tier.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • A trigger is the start of the chain. A new customer opportunity still needs a testable proposition. State whose problem your choice addresses and what evidence would show demand.
  • Resources come with constraints. An established brand, relationships and distribution can help a venture. Explain which of those assets transfer to your proposed market and which assumptions need testing.
  • Connect experimentation to support. Identify a customer experiment, the time and discretion it needs, and the result a manager would use to decide what happens next.
⚙️

Operation Lead

Selling computing means owning the computers before anyone asks for them, and Amazon's own filing warns that adding new businesses makes its network "increasingly complex." How much infrastructure do you build for demand that doesn't exist yet?

  • A Add capacity only after demand actually appears — I'm not buying servers on a hope.
  • B Start on our own spare capacity from the retail peaks, then build dedicated capacity as real demand shows up.
  • C Build ahead of demand and accept low utilisation early — if a customer can't get capacity on demand, we don't have a product.
  • D Lease from existing data centre providers until the demand is proven, then build.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Scaling turns an idea into commitments. Connect your capacity decision to the lecture's move from experimentation to scale. Explain what must be committed before the outcome is known.
  • Compare the costs of being wrong. Consider both excess capacity and unmet demand, including their effects on the parent and on customers. State which assumptions drive your judgment.
  • Make the review process concrete. Identify who can authorize a capacity change, how quickly they can act and what evidence would trigger a different plan.
💻

Tech Lead

AWS is being built out of the infrastructure Amazon already runs its own store on. The filing describes S3 as providing "a simple web services interface for storing and retrieving data from anywhere on the web." What do you build, and what do you buy?

  • A Expose the systems that already run the retail site directly — they work, and it's the fastest way to ship.
  • B Build them as proper documented services with one interface — and make our own teams use exactly the same one outsiders do.
  • C Build for outside customers first and move our internal teams onto it later, once it's proven.
  • D White-label infrastructure from an existing hosting provider and put our interface on top.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Architecture involves organizational boundaries. Technical choices distribute control among the venture, parent teams and suppliers. Explain which decisions your team can make and where it needs agreement.
  • Connect capabilities to the opportunity. The Gmail example used resources inside an established company. Identify which existing capabilities your proposal uses and what new capability it must develop.
  • Plan for experimentation and integration. Explain how your choice would be tested, whose work would change and how incentives could help or hinder adoption.
🔒

Security Lead

You are about to hold other companies' data and run their code on hardware you also use for Amazon's own retail business. Some of those customers will be Amazon's competitors. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Cap our liability in the contract and make clear customers secure their own data.
  • B Stand up a full compliance and certification programme now, before we take a serious customer.
  • C Defer the serious security spend until the revenue justifies it.
  • D Treat separating customers on shared hardware as the top risk, and publish exactly what we secure and what the customer secures.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • A new venture changes the stakeholder map. Identify whose data, operations and reputation your decision affects. Explain which assumptions from the parent business need reconsideration.
  • Discretion needs clear boundaries. The CEAI chain asks where permission runs out. Describe who can accept a risk, who reviews that decision and when concerns reach a manager.
  • Evaluate outcomes beyond launch. Explain how you would assess customer confidence and service reliability alongside growth, and what evidence would change your allocation of resources.

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question. After the five leads present, consider funding, capacity, customer data, customers, and the platform.

  • 1 Rank the problems 1–5 by what determines whether the venture still exists in three years. Use each once. Justify #1 in one sentence containing a figure from your packet.
  • 2 Allocate 100 points across the five problems in blocks of 10, totalling exactly 100.
  • 3 Name what it needs from its parent. What does this venture most need from Amazon that it cannot get for itself: money, permission, time, or cover from the quarterly review? Explain why.
🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Priorities express a judgment. Ranking states order; weighting states relative emphasis. Explain your criteria and the tradeoffs they create over the venture's three-year horizon.
  • Trace dependencies through the CEAI chain. Connect the team's choices to management support, discretion, rewards, time and boundaries. Identify who can change a missing condition.
  • Separate access from authority. The parent may possess resources the venture cannot allocate itself. Explain what your plan requires from Amazon and how progress would be assessed.

Key takeaway

Trace the decision through the CEAI chain: trigger → internal conditions → middle manager → outcomes. Explain how the parent's resources and constraints affect your plan, who can authorize it, and how you would assess progress.
